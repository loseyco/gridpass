## 2. Logic Chain

The observations above lead directly to the following security and operational conclusions:

1. **Observation 1 & 2 Conflict (Operational Lockout)**:
   - If a pass is generated and downloaded 24 hours before gate arrival (Observation 1), the timestamp stored in the pass (`checked_in_timestamp` or similar) will be 24 hours old.
   - If the scanner rejects any pass whose timestamp is older than 30 minutes (Observation 2), **100% of legitimate pre-registered passes will be rejected as stale.**
   - *Conclusion*: Pre-arrival downloads are completely incompatible with the 30-minute validity window, leading to a system-wide denial of service at gate check-in.

2. **Observation 3 Cryptographic Loophole (Waiver Bypass)**:
   - Storing the "first 8 characters" of a hex-encoded SHA256 hash means the entropy is limited to $8 \text{ hex characters} \times 4 \text{ bits/char} = 32 \text{ bits}$ of actual hash data.
   - A 32-bit collision space is highly vulnerable to birthday-attack brute-forcing ($2^{16} = 65,536$ trials).
   - An active passenger or banned driver can easily brute-force their registration name input (e.g. by adding whitespace) to match the 8-character prefix of a valid signed waiver in the local database.
   - *Conclusion*: Attenders can bypass legal liability waivers entirely by exploiting prefix collisions, invalidating the legal compliance guarantees of the system.
   - Furthermore, a string of 8 characters consumes **8 bytes** in Protobuf. If changed to raw `bytes` of size 8, it would provide **64 bits of entropy** (collision-resistant) for the *exact same* serialization size!

3. **Observation 5 Cryptographic Loophole (Trial Verification DoS)**:
   - The outer `SignedSecurePass` envelope (Observation 5) does not include a key identifier or version.
   - Under key rotation or multi-venue deployment, the offline scanner must support multiple active public keys.
   - Without a key ID, the scanner must execute "trial verification" (trying every active key) or parse untrusted `serialized_metadata` first to find the key ID, violating the "verify before parsing" rule.
   - *Conclusion*: A flood of malformed scans forces the terminal into expensive cryptographic calculations, creating a CPU denial-of-service vector that breaks the <5-second entry SLA.

4. **Observation 6 Terminology Conflict**:
   - Ed25519 signature verification is a non-encrypting mathematical verification. The statement "decrypt and verify" (Observation 6) is a technical mismatch that can lead developers to implement insecure encryption schemes or custom decrypters, risking compatibility and size overhead.

## 4. Conclusion

Based on the logic chain, the specification is **BLOCKED** due to:
1. **Critical Operational Flaw**: Pre-arrival caching is broken by the 30-minute validity window.
2. **High Security Loophole**: Truncated 32-bit waiver hashes permit trivial legal waiver circumvention.
3. **Medium DoS Vulnerability**: Missing key identifiers in the outer envelope trigger trial verification CPU spikes.

### Actionable Remediations Required:
1. **Redefine Pass Lifecycles**: Validate pre-arrival passes for the duration of the event (12–24h) and secure them against screenshots via the **double-scan replay cache** and **Screenshot Evasion Guards** (visual plate/name matching).
2. **Convert Hex String to Raw Bytes**: Change `passenger_waiver_hashes` to `repeated bytes passenger_waiver_hashes = 10;` and store 8 bytes of raw binary, elevating entropy to 64 bits with 0% size overhead.
3. **Add Key Identifier**: Add `uint32 signing_key_id = 3;` to the outer `SignedSecurePass` envelope to prevent trial verification and maintain the "verify before parsing" boundary.
4. **Correct Terminology**: Replace references to "decrypt and verify" with "decode the outer envelope and verify the signature."

Assert whether the offline scanner's validation function rejects the pass as expired under the 30-minute limit.
